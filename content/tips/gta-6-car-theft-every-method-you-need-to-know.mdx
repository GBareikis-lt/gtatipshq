## Stealing Cars Is No Longer One-Size-Fits-All

In previous GTA titles, grabbing a vehicle was straightforward: smash the window, hop in, drive away. GTA 6 is changing that formula significantly, tying the theft method directly to the type of car you're targeting. This fits neatly into Rockstar's broader push for realism — the same philosophy that shaped Red Dead Redemption 2's immersive systems — and it adds a meaningful layer of strategy to one of the series' most iconic activities.

## Older Cars: Classic Techniques Still Work

If you're going after an older vehicle, you have a couple of options:

- **Smash and grab** – The classic window-break approach returns for legacy cars, quick but noisy and likely to attract attention.
- **Slim jim** – For a stealthier entry, you can use a slim jim (the same tool locksmiths use for lockouts). This involves a short minigame using your controller's analog stick. Once inside, you'll still need to hotwire the car before driving off.

Choosing between speed and stealth will matter depending on your situation — whether you're evading police or trying to stay under the radar.

## Newer Vehicles: You'll Need a Key Cloner

Modern cars — including what sounds like GTA 6's inevitable electric-vehicle parody — can't be cracked with a slim jim or a quick hotwire. For these, you'll need a **key cloner**, a device that replicates the car's electronic key signal. This points to a more involved process, though full details on exactly how the minigame or acquisition works are still emerging.

> This mechanic rewards preparation. Knowing what car you want before a mission and having the right tools ready could make the difference between a clean getaway and a chaotic chase.
