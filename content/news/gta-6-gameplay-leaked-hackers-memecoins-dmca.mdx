## What Happened

Several minutes of footage purporting to show GTA 6 gameplay surfaced online this week, roughly three months ahead of the game's [November 19, 2026 release](/news). While Rockstar Games has yet to make an official statement, parent company Take-Two Interactive moved quickly to issue DMCA takedown notices against the clips — a strong signal that the footage is genuine.

## Who Is Behind the Leak

The group claiming responsibility posted a manifesto — since taken down — framing the leak as a protest against GTA 6's physical console edition reportedly being a code-in-a-box release rather than a disc containing the full game. They also warned that other developers shipping games without a proper physical edition could be targeted next.

However, the footage itself was overlaid with advertisements for a memecoin and requests for donations, which has cast serious doubt on the stated consumer-rights motivation. Critics have pointed out that promoting a speculative cryptocurrency alongside a high-profile stolen leak looks far more like opportunism than activism.

## Stop Killing Games Weighs In

The campaign group Stop Killing Games — known for pushing back against publishers shutting down online titles — distanced itself entirely from the hackers and urged the community not to support them. The group warned that actions like this can harm the thousands of developers working on large projects, most of whom have no say in packaging decisions.

> Stop Killing Games made clear that leaking proprietary footage and monetising it via memecoins is not a legitimate form of consumer advocacy.

## What This Means for Players

- The leak has not changed the official release date or any confirmed details about GTA 6.
- Sharing or watching DMCA-flagged clips carries potential legal risk.
- Treat any "confirmed" details drawn from unverified leaked footage with caution — context can be missing or misleading.
