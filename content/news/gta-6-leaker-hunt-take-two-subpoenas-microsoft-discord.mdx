## Take-Two Goes Legal to Track Down GTA 6 Leakers

Take-Two Interactive has escalated its response to ongoing GTA 6 leaks by issuing legal subpoenas to both Microsoft and Discord. The move signals that the publisher is serious about identifying whoever has been responsible for the steady drip of unreleased game information reaching the public ahead of the title's [November 2026 launch](/news).

Subpoenas of this kind typically compel companies to hand over account data, communication logs, or other records that could help lawyers trace the origin of leaked material. Targeting Microsoft — which owns platforms like Xbox services and GitHub — alongside Discord, a hub for gaming communities, suggests investigators believe leaked content may have been shared or coordinated through those services.

## Why This Matters

This is not the first time Rockstar's parent company has pursued legal action around GTA 6. A major hack in 2022 led to criminal charges against a suspect in the UK. The new subpoenas indicate a fresh wave of leaks has prompted another round of enforcement:

- **Microsoft** could be asked to provide data tied to Xbox accounts, OneDrive storage, or developer tools.
- **Discord** may be required to share server logs or direct-message records linked to specific users.
- Both companies have privacy policies, but valid legal subpoenas generally override those protections in the relevant jurisdiction.

> Notably, reports indicate that yet another GTA 6 leak surfaced on the same day the subpoenas became public knowledge — suggesting the hunt is far from over.

## What It Means for Fans

For players eager to follow GTA 6 news, it's worth being cautious about engaging with leaked material. Sharing or spreading unverified leaks could, in theory, put community members in awkward legal territory. With the game now confirmed for **November 19, 2026**, official reveals are likely to come thick and fast anyway — making the wait for legitimate news much shorter than it once was.
