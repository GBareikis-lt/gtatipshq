## The GTA IV Soundtrack Warning

Back in 2018, a significant chunk of Grand Theft Auto IV's beloved radio stations went silent. Rockstar had originally licensed those tracks for roughly ten years, and when the agreements expired, the songs were pulled from the game rather than renewed. It was a stark reminder that the music we enjoy in GTA titles isn't owned outright — it's rented.

With GTA 6 set to launch on **November 19, 2026**, and already carrying enormous expectations as one of the biggest entertainment releases ever, a fair question is circulating in the community: did Rockstar learn from that situation and lock down longer licences this time?

## What We Actually Know

Honestly, very little — at least publicly. Rockstar has not made any statements about the length of its music licensing agreements for GTA 6. The question was raised in a [r/GTA6 thread](https://www.reddit.com/r/GTA6/comments/1w7ogse/will_music_last_longer/) and is purely speculative at this stage. No insider leaks or official documents have surfaced to clarify the situation.

## Why It Matters

- **Replay value** — GTA titles are played for decades. Losing tracks years after launch genuinely degrades the experience.
- **Scale of GTA 6** — The game's radio soundtrack is expected to be massive. More songs means more individual licencing deals to manage.
- **Streaming era complications** — Rights negotiations have only become more complex since 2008, involving labels, publishers, and sometimes individual artists.

> This is unconfirmed community speculation. Until Rockstar or Take-Two makes an official statement, treat any claims about licencing terms as rumour only.
