## Leaker Continues Despite Legal Pressure

The individual believed to be behind a series of unauthorised GTA 6 gameplay videos has posted two additional clips, seemingly undeterred by the significant legal heat now surrounding the situation. Take-Two Interactive — Rockstar's parent company — has reportedly issued subpoenas to both Microsoft and Discord in an effort to uncover the leaker's identity.

Subpoenas of this kind are a formal legal mechanism that compels a third-party service to hand over user data. By targeting Microsoft and Discord specifically, Take-Two appears to be tracing accounts or communications linked to whoever is leaking this footage.

## What This Means for GTA 6 Fans

For most players, the legal back-and-forth won't affect the experience of [GTA 6](/news) when it launches on **November 19, 2026**. However, there are a few things worth keeping in mind:

- **Spoiler risk is rising.** Each new leak adds more unfinished or out-of-context footage to the internet. If you want to go in fresh, now is a good time to be selective about what you watch.
- **Leaked footage is pre-release and unpolished.** Builds shown in leaks rarely represent the final product — animations, textures, and mechanics can all change significantly before launch.
- **Engaging with leaks carries risk.** Re-sharing or widely distributing leaked material can attract legal attention, as Take-Two has shown it is actively pursuing this case.

## Take-Two's Track Record

This isn't the first time Take-Two has pursued legal action over GTA leaks. The company took decisive steps following the major 2022 GTA 6 source code breach, ultimately seeing the individual responsible face serious legal consequences. The current situation suggests the publisher intends to pursue this matter with equal determination.

> As always, treat any leaked footage as unofficial and potentially misleading — wait for Rockstar's own reveals for anything definitive.
