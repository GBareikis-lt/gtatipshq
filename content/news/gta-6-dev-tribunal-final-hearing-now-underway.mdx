## Final Hearing Gets Underway in Scotland

The long-running employment dispute between Rockstar Games and 31 developers who were dismissed from the studio last October has reached its concluding stage. The final tribunal hearing opened in Scotland this week, drawing attention not just inside the courtroom but outside it too, where supporters gathered for rallies and speeches in solidarity with the former employees.

The case, which has been followed closely by games industry observers, centres on whether those workers were treated fairly when they were let go — reportedly marched out of the office without much notice. The IWGB union, which has been supporting the claimants throughout the process, framed the opening of the final hearing as a significant moment for workers' rights in the games industry.

## What This Means for the Industry

While the tribunal's outcome won't directly affect the development of GTA 6 — which remains on course for its [November 2026 launch](/news) — the case has become a touchstone for broader conversations about employment conditions at major studios. Key points that have emerged during the dispute include:

- Questions around how and why the redundancies were handled
- Whether proper consultation processes were followed
- The role of union representation in games development workplaces

> This is one of the more high-profile employment cases the UK and Scottish games industry has seen in recent years, and its outcome could set a precedent for how studios handle workforce changes going forward.

## What Happens Next

With the final hearing now open, a ruling is expected at some point after proceedings conclude. We'll report on the outcome once a decision is reached. In the meantime, the case serves as a reminder that behind every major release like GTA 6, there are real people whose working lives are directly shaped by how studios operate.
