## RDR2 as a Blueprint for GTA 6 HDR

One discussion gaining traction in the GTA 6 community centres on something most players rarely think about: HDR implementation. A Reddit user on r/GTA6 recently argued that, based on Rockstar's work with *Red Dead Redemption 2*, GTA 6 could end up being the gold standard for HDR in gaming.

The logic is straightforward. RDR2 launched in 2018 on PlayStation 4 hardware, yet its HDR support — added via a post-launch patch in 2019 — remains among the most praised in any console game. Sunrises over the frontier, candle-lit saloons, and thunderstorms all benefit noticeably when the setting is properly calibrated. If Rockstar could achieve that on last-generation hardware, the argument goes, a next-gen title built from the ground up for modern displays should be something else entirely.

## Why HDR Takes Real Effort to Get Right

It's worth noting that slapping an HDR label on a game does not automatically improve the picture. Proper HDR implementation requires artists and engineers to work in high dynamic range throughout development — not just map existing assets to a wider brightness range at the end. That distinction matters, and it's part of why so many games with "HDR" support still look washed-out or flat on capable displays.

Given Rockstar's reputation for visual polish, there's a reasonable case that GTA 6's sun-drenched Vice City setting could be a showcase for what modern HDR displays are capable of.

## Getting the Most from It at Home

> To see GTA 6's HDR as intended, look for HGiG (HDR Gaming Interest Group) mode on your TV or monitor and calibrate your display before launching the game.

- **HGiG mode** tells the game exactly what your display can handle, rather than guessing.
- Playing in SDR or with uncalibrated HDR can make the image look worse than intended.
- Check your TV's picture settings now — many sets bury HDR and HGiG options in advanced menus.
