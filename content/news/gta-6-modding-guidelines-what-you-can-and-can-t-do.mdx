## Rockstar Sets the Ground Rules for GTA 6 Mods

Rockstar Games has released an updated set of modding guidelines covering GTA 6 and its older titles. While the studio has generally tolerated single-player mods for years, these rules make the boundaries more explicit than ever — and there are a few restrictions that creative modders will want to note carefully.

## What the Guidelines Actually Say

The rules cover several distinct areas:

- **Story and characters** — Mods must not expand, alter, or build upon Rockstar's official storylines, missions, or characters. Fan continuations of the GTA 6 narrative are off the table.
- **Cross-title content** — Porting maps, assets, or characters between Rockstar titles (think: putting Vice City into GTA 5, or vice versa) is explicitly prohibited.
- **Multiplayer** — Any online or multiplayer modding must go through Rockstar's official licensed Creator Platform. Cheat menus and tools that interfere with online services remain banned.
- **Platform portability** — You cannot modify a Rockstar game to run on hardware or platforms where it was not officially released.
- **Voiceovers and IP** — Reusing voice performances from any Rockstar game is not allowed, and mods must not imply official endorsement through branding or logos.
- **Third-party IP** — Real-world brands, music, artwork, and other protected material belonging to outside parties cannot appear in mods.

## What This Means for the Modding Community

> These guidelines reinforce that single-player, original-content mods remain the safest creative space — Rockstar has not signalled a blanket ban on modding itself.

For most hobbyist modders, the day-to-day impact is limited. New gameplay mechanics, visual overhauls, and original custom missions that stay within the GTA 6 world are not targeted by any of these rules. The restrictions are aimed squarely at story expansions, cross-game asset transfers, and anything touching multiplayer.
