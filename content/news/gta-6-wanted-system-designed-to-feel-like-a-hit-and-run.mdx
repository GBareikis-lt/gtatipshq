## A Smarter, More Immersive Wanted System

Rockstar Games has confirmed that losing the cops in GTA 6 will be a far more nuanced experience than simply sprinting out of a highlighted circle on the map. Speaking to IGN during a 30-minute gameplay demo shown ahead of the Netflix Extended Look in August, co-studio head and head of development Rob Nelson explained that the team wants escaping the law to carry the same tense, unpredictable energy as a real hit-and-run situation — where the heat doesn't just evaporate the moment you cross an invisible boundary.

While exact mechanical details are still limited, the message is clear: players will need to think more creatively about how they shake off pursuing officers in the state of Leonida.

## Safehouses, NPCs, and a Living World

The demo also shed light on several other systems that feed into this richer gameplay loop:

- **Safehouses** serve as interactive personal spaces where Jason and Lucia can swap characters, customise their surroundings, and take part in unique context-sensitive interactions. Rockstar was quick to note these aren't properties the duo own — they're crashing in someone else's home.
- **NPC interactions** have been significantly deepened, building on the foundation laid by Red Dead Redemption 2. Downtown Leonida is populated with a wide range of characters — from ordinary joggers to more dangerous residents — each capable of reacting in more complex and varied ways than anything seen in previous GTA titles.

> This layered approach to both the wanted system and everyday NPC encounters suggests Rockstar is pushing hard for moment-to-moment gameplay to feel genuinely reactive rather than scripted.

## What This Means for Players

If the wanted level truly behaves with the logic and persistence of a real police response, expect stealth, disguise, and environmental awareness to matter more than ever. Classic tactics like swapping cars or hiding in an alley may still help, but blending into Leonida's bustling world could become just as important.
