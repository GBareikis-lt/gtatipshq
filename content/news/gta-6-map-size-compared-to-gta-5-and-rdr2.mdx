## A New Scale for Rockstar's Open Worlds

Rockstar Games has always pushed the boundaries of open-world size, but GTA 6's map — set across the sun-baked state of Leonida — reportedly raises the bar higher than anything the studio has shipped before. That means it surpasses both the sprawling frontier of *Red Dead Redemption 2* and the sun-soaked streets of *GTA 5*'s Los Santos, two benchmarks fans have used for years when debating open-world scale.

## How the Numbers Break Down

While exact square-mile figures are still being debated by the community, early analysis suggests Leonida dwarfs its predecessors by a meaningful margin:

- **GTA 5 / San Andreas** — a dense urban core with surrounding hills and countryside, long considered one of gaming's largest city maps.
- **Red Dead Redemption 2** — wider and more rural, emphasising natural terrain over urban density; previously Rockstar's biggest achievement in sheer landmass.
- **GTA 6 / Leonida** — reportedly combines the urban complexity of GTA 5 with expansive wilderness regions, giving it more total area than either predecessor.

> Keep in mind that raw size isn't everything — how that space is filled with activities, detail, and variety matters just as much. Early impressions suggest Leonida scores well on all three fronts.

## What This Means for Players

A larger map has real gameplay implications heading into the [November 19, 2026 launch](/news):

- Exploration will take longer, rewarding players who enjoy organic discovery.
- Driving and flying routes between key locations will feel genuinely epic in scale.
- Online modes should have far more room for large-scale events and territory play.
