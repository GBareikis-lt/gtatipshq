## The P2P Problem That Has Plagued GTA Online

Anyone who has spent serious time in GTA Online knows the frustration: a brief blip in your connection and suddenly the entire lobby appears to vanish, forcing you back to a solo session. The root cause is GTA Online's peer-to-peer (P2P) networking model, where player machines effectively host the session rather than a centralised Rockstar server. A single bad connection — yours or someone else's — can destabilise the whole lobby.

Red Dead Online runs on a similar architecture, so this isn't a one-off oversight; it's been Rockstar's long-standing approach to multiplayer infrastructure.

## Could GTA 6 Online Change the Model?

As of now, Rockstar has made **no official announcement** about the server architecture behind GTA 6 Online. Everything below is community speculation and informed guesswork:

- **Dedicated servers** would mean a Rockstar-owned machine hosts each session, dramatically reducing the impact of any one player's packet loss.
- Several major live-service competitors — including *Fortnite* and *Destiny 2* — already use dedicated servers as standard.
- The jump to a new engine and a reported expanded Online mode could give Rockstar the opportunity to overhaul networking from the ground up.
- Hosting dedicated servers at scale is expensive, but Take-Two's ongoing GTA Online revenue suggests the budget exists.

> **Keep in mind:** Until Rockstar speaks publicly about GTA 6 Online infrastructure, all of this remains unconfirmed rumour and fan discussion.

## What It Would Mean for Players

If Rockstar does move to dedicated servers for GTA 6 Online, the practical benefits would be significant: more stable sessions, fairer host advantage, and far fewer phantom disconnects. For players already dealing with imperfect home connections, it could make the difference between a playable experience and constant frustration.
