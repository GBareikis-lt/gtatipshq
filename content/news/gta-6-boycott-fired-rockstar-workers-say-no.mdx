## Former Rockstar Staff Speak Out on Boycott Calls

A group of former Rockstar Games employees — currently in a legal dispute with the studio — have publicly asked fans **not** to boycott GTA 6 on their behalf. The message came amid growing calls from some corners of the community to skip the game as a form of protest in solidarity with the workers.

The fired staff made clear that a sales boycott is not what they want, and that there are more constructive ways for fans to stand behind them while still holding the company accountable.

## What They're Asking Fans to Do Instead

While the workers did not lay out an exhaustive list of alternative actions, the core message was consistent: purchasing GTA 6 and supporting the thousands of developers who worked on it is not at odds with also caring about fair treatment in the games industry. Some broader suggestions that align with their position include:

- **Follow and amplify** the workers' own statements and legal updates through official channels
- **Engage with labour advocacy** organisations focused on games industry workers' rights
- **Stay informed** rather than reacting to second-hand narratives about the dispute

> The workers' own voices are the most reliable source here — seek out their direct statements rather than relying on social media summaries.

## What This Means for GTA 6

The game itself remains on track for its **November 19, 2026** launch. This dispute is a separate matter from development, and the vast majority of the enormous team behind GTA 6 are unaffected by the legal case.

For fans weighing up the situation, the clearest takeaway is straightforward: the people at the centre of this controversy have explicitly said a boycott doesn't help them. Supporting their cause through awareness and advocacy appears to be the path they prefer.
