## Why GTA 6 Is Skipping PC at Launch

If you're a PC player waiting to jump into GTA 6, you already know the pain: the game is launching on consoles first, with no confirmed PC release window yet. It's a decision that has frustrated many fans, but a former Rockstar producer is offering some clarity on why it works this way.

John Ricchio, who worked at Rockstar during earlier GTA titles, explained to VideoGamesChronicle that the console-first approach isn't a deliberate snub of the PC community. Instead, it comes down to straightforward development logic.

## The Case for Console-First Development

According to Ricchio, consoles offer something PC simply can't: a fixed, predictable hardware target. When every player is running the same CPU, GPU, and memory configuration, a development team can optimise aggressively without worrying about thousands of possible hardware combinations.

The general workflow, as he describes it, looks something like this:

- Lock down performance and visuals against console hardware specs
- Ship a polished, stable product to a massive audience
- Use that breathing room to properly adapt the game for PC's enormous hardware variety

This mirrors what Rockstar did with both GTA V and Red Dead Redemption 2, which both arrived on PC well after their console debuts.

## What This Means for GTA 6 Players

> The silver lining for PC players: a later port often means a better-optimised release, with extra time to iron out driver issues and add higher-fidelity settings.
