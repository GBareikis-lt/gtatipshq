## The Theory Making Rounds on Reddit

With GTA 6 still a couple of months away, fans are dissecting every confirmed detail with increasing intensity. One post on r/GTA6 caught attention recently, raising an interesting question: could some of the game's touted NPC behaviors actually be optimization techniques dressed up as features?

The argument centers on a detail mentioned in an IGN interview — that NPCs in GTA 6 will notice if the player is following or eavesdropping on them, and may even turn aggressive. A self-described programmer behind the post argues this mechanic could serve a dual purpose: it creates an immersive, reactive world on the surface, while quietly limiting how long and how closely the game needs to simulate any individual NPC's full daily routine.

## The RDR2 Comparison

The post uses Red Dead Redemption 2 as its benchmark. RDR2 wowed players by giving NPCs genuinely detailed schedules — waking, working, socializing, and sleeping in believable loops. It set a bar that few open-world games have matched since.

The suggestion here is that rather than simulating that same depth for every NPC in the far larger and denser world of GTA 6, Rockstar may have built in a natural "escape valve": once an NPC clocks you watching them, the interaction changes, reducing the need to maintain a fully detailed simulation beneath the surface.

## Worth Taking With a Pinch of Salt

> This remains unconfirmed speculation from a Reddit user, not a verified analysis from anyone with inside knowledge of Rockstar's engine or development process.

That said, it's a genuinely thoughtful read on game development trade-offs. Optimization and immersion aren't mutually exclusive, and Rockstar has a long history of making technical constraints feel like intentional design.
