## Sheriff Walks Back Broader Opposition

Miami-Dade Sheriff Rosie Cordero-Stutz made headlines earlier when she described Grand Theft Auto as a franchise centred on criminal activity, leading many to assume she opposed the NBA's high-profile Vice City-themed branding push entirely. Her more recent comments tell a different story.

According to Cordero-Stutz, her objection was always narrowly focused: she does not want GTA 6 promotional material tied to publicly owned buildings, roads, or other government-operated infrastructure. Private venues and commercial partnerships, including the NBA's own Vice City makeover, are another matter altogether — and not something she intends to fight.

## What the NBA Vice City Deal Actually Is

Rockstar and the NBA struck a collaboration that wraps Miami-area basketball branding in heavy Vice City aesthetics ahead of GTA 6's November 2026 launch. Think retro pastel colour schemes, neon signage, and the kind of sun-soaked imagery that has defined the GTA 6 marketing campaign so far. It is a bold commercial tie-in, and for the most part the local reception has been positive.

## Why It Matters for GTA 6 Hype

This clarification is worth noting for a few reasons:

- It signals that the real-world pushback against GTA 6 marketing is more limited in scope than early reporting suggested.
- Large-scale commercial promotions in and around Miami are unlikely to face official interference.
- The Vice City brand continues to build mainstream visibility ahead of the game's release.

> Keep in mind this is a local political story, not a Rockstar announcement — it does not affect the game itself or its confirmed release date.
