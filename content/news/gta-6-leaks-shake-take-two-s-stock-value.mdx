## A Turbulent Week for Take-Two

It has been a rough stretch for Rockstar's parent company. Take-Two Interactive's share price slipped from roughly $245 at the start of the week to around $236 as alleged GTA 6 gameplay footage spread rapidly across the internet. That decline translates to billions of dollars wiped from the company's total market capitalisation — not a welcome headline just days before a major planned reveal.

## What's Driving the Drop?

Several factors appear to be piling up at once:

- **Widespread leaks** — Footage described as early or unfinished GTA 6 gameplay has been circulating heavily online, generating headlines well beyond gaming media and even drawing commentary from US political figures.
- **CEO share sale** — Take-Two boss Strauss Zelnick recently sold approximately $10 million worth of company stock, a move that often draws scrutiny from investors regardless of the reason.
- **Quarterly earnings** — Take-Two's most recent earnings report revealed a net loss of more than $30 million over the past three months, alongside a drop in net bookings.

On a more positive note for the company, the Ontario Teachers' Pension Plan disclosed a purchase of nearly 150,000 Take-Two shares worth over $37 million, signalling that some institutional investors still see long-term value.

## The Bigger Picture for GTA 6 Fans

> Take-Two's stock volatility doesn't change what matters most: GTA 6 is still on track for its **November 19, 2026** release on PS5 and Xbox Series X|S.

An extended trailer is reportedly arriving on Netflix on August 27, which could shift the narrative quickly. Stock prices fluctuate for many reasons, and a well-received official reveal may help stabilise investor sentiment.
