## Rockstar Says No to MTX and AI

In a welcome piece of news for fans, Rockstar North co-studio head Rob Nelson has directly addressed two of the hottest topics surrounding GTA 6: microtransactions and generative AI. During a recent visit to Rockstar's Edinburgh studio, journalists from Kinda Funny — Andy Cortez and Greg Miller — put both questions to Nelson point blank, and the answer in each case was a straightforward "no."

Cortez confirmed that players will not be able to spend real money on in-game purchases at launch, while Miller relayed that generative AI played no part in the game's development. The New York Times separately corroborated the no-AI stance in its own GTA 6 feature published around the same time.

## A Handcrafted Game at Massive Scale

The absence of generative AI is perhaps even more striking when you consider the sheer scale of what Rockstar has built. According to figures shared alongside the studio visits, GTA 6 contains over 600,000 animations — a staggering leap from the roughly 55,000 found in GTA 5 and the 300,000 in Red Dead Redemption 2. Every one of those animations was produced by human artists, underlining just how much traditional craft has gone into this project.

## What This Means for Players

- **No pay-to-win or cosmetic storefronts** at launch, meaning the experience should feel complete out of the box.
- **No AI-generated assets**, which fans of Rockstar's attention to detail will appreciate — the world, characters, and animations are all the product of the studio's own teams.
- These commitments apply specifically to **launch**; Rockstar has not made long-term promises about GTA Online's future monetization model.

> Keep in mind that the no-microtransaction confirmation covers the base game at release. Given the history of GTA Online, it is reasonable to expect that the online component may introduce its own economy down the line.
