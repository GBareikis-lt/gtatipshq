## Rockstar's Extraordinary Security Measures

Rockstar Games has lifted the lid on just how far it goes to protect GTA 6 from leaks — and the answer is further than most people imagined. In an opening statement submitted to an ongoing employment tribunal, the studio disclosed that it was compelled to install privacy film across the windows of its office buildings after individuals used drones to photograph developers' screens and workspaces from outside.

The tribunal itself concerns 31 developers Rockstar dismissed last year, but the security details buried inside the filing paint a vivid picture of the pressure the studio operates under as it prepares for one of the most anticipated game launches in history.

## Why This Level of Secrecy?

With GTA 6 set to launch on **November 19, 2026**, Rockstar is in the final stretch of development. The studio has long maintained tight information control, but drone-assisted surveillance of office windows represents an escalation that even seasoned industry watchers might find surprising. It underlines just how intense public and media interest in the game has become.

- Privacy film was reportedly installed as a direct response to confirmed drone incidents
- The disclosure came through legal proceedings, not a voluntary announcement
- Rockstar has historically been among the most secretive major studios in the industry

> These measures reflect how seriously Rockstar treats the security of an unreleased title that carries enormous commercial and cultural weight.

## What It Means for Fans

For players eagerly tracking every scrap of GTA 6 information, this is a reminder that credible leaks are genuinely hard to come by — anything circulating online should be treated with healthy scepticism. Rockstar's security posture suggests the studio remains firmly in control of its own narrative ahead of release.
