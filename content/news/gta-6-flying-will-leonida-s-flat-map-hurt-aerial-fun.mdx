## The Topography Problem

One of the quieter conversations building around GTA 6 concerns something pilots in GTA V took for granted: elevation. Rockstar's last open world gave players the San Andreas mountain range, deep canyons, and steep ridgelines that turned every low-altitude flight into genuine navigation. Leonida, modelled closely on Florida, is famously one of the flattest places in North America.

That has some fans wondering whether cruising from Vice City out to the rural stretches of Port Gellhorn will simply mean pointing a nose forward and waiting — beautiful, perhaps, but not especially demanding.

## What Could Keep It Interesting

Flat terrain doesn't automatically mean boring skies. A few factors could compensate:

- **Dense urban airspace** — Vice City's skyline alone should create low-level obstacle courses through towers, bridges, and elevated highways.
- **Weather systems** — Florida is hurricane country. If Rockstar leans into dynamic storms, turbulence, and reduced visibility, pilots will have plenty to manage even over open water.
- **The Florida Keys corridor** — island-hopping at wave height over open ocean is a different kind of challenge from mountain-dodging, and potentially just as exciting visually.
- **Air traffic and wanted levels** — heavier civilian and law-enforcement air presence could replace geographical hazards with human ones.

## Speculation, Not Confirmation

> It's worth stressing that none of this is confirmed. The full extent of Leonida's terrain, weather simulation depth, or air-traffic density hasn't been detailed by Rockstar. These are community observations based on the trailers and real-world geography.
