## Old Leak, New Attention

A batch of files has resurfaced online that appears to originate from the large-scale Rockstar Games data breach in 2023. According to reporting from PC Gamer, the material includes what looks like an early iteration of the GTA 6 map, alongside evidence of scrapped GTA 5 content — including a Liberty City expansion and a planned "Agent Trevor" DLC that never made it to players.

The exact source of the files remains unverified, and it's worth stressing that none of this is confirmed or endorsed by Rockstar Games or Take-Two Interactive. Treat everything here as unconfirmed.

## What the Files Reportedly Show

- An early map surface for GTA 6, likely representing a work-in-progress state from several years ago
- Design remnants of a Liberty City DLC for GTA 5 that was ultimately cancelled
- References to an "Agent Trevor" project that never shipped

Because these assets are from an early development stage, they may look very different from what players will experience when GTA 6 launches on **November 19, 2026**. Early map geometry and layouts frequently change dramatically before a game ships.

## Why This Matters — and Why to Be Cautious

Leaked development assets can offer a fascinating window into how a game evolves, but they can also mislead. An early map build says little about the final product's size, detail, or layout. Rockstar is known for iterating heavily throughout development.

> Take leaked visuals and map data with a healthy dose of scepticism — what ships in November 2026 could look dramatically different.
