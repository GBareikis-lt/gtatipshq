## Rockstar Went to Extraordinary Lengths for Authenticity

Building the fictional state of Leonida wasn't purely a desk job for Rockstar's artists. According to a Bloomberg interview with artist Aaron Garbut, the team made multiple research trips to Miami — riding along with police officers, visiting real nightclubs, and spending time with the people who work in them. That hands-on approach fed directly into how the game's party venues look, sound, and feel.

## What Makes These Nightclubs Stand Out

The result of all that fieldwork goes well beyond cosmetic polish. Garbut describes the final product as having "layers and layers and layers of detail," and the specifics back that up:

- **Individual NPC behaviour** — each patron has their own distinct animations and scripted routines rather than a shared pool of generic actions.
- **A fully stocked bar** — drinks aren't just decorative; the bar features a variety of unique beverages.
- **Music-reactive lighting** — perhaps the most striking touch, the club's lighting rigs pulse and shift in sync with whatever track is playing at that moment.

> This level of environmental detail suggests nightclubs could serve as more than backdrop scenery — they may function as genuine social hubs, especially if GTA 6 Online leans into them the way GTA Online eventually did.

## Why This Matters for Players

Previous GTA titles treated nightclubs as largely passive spaces. If Rockstar has built venues with reactive atmospheres and individually scripted crowds, these locations could become memorable hangout spots rather than one-time story stops. It also raises the bar for what players can expect from Leonida's open world more broadly — if this level of care went into a single venue type, the rest of the [world](/map) likely benefits from the same philosophy.
