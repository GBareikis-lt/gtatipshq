## A Weather Engine Built from the Ground Up

Rockstar Games has confirmed that GTA 6 features a completely rebuilt weather system, engineered specifically to give the open world a sense of scale that the studio says it has never achieved before. Speaking to Eurogamer, the team described going back and re-engineering the core weather technology rather than simply upgrading what was used in previous titles — including the already impressive storm effects seen in Red Dead Redemption 2.

## What to Expect in the Skies Over Leonida

The overhaul introduces some genuinely dramatic atmospheric events and visual details:

- **Hurricanes** — large-scale tropical storms that should make Leonida's coastal setting feel authentically dangerous
- **Rainbows** — a small but grounding touch that adds realism to the moments after heavy downpours
- **Cumulonimbus clouds** — towering, vertigo-inducing storm formations that reshape the skyline in real time
- **Heavy rain** — more convincing and immersive than anything Rockstar has rendered before

Taken together, these elements suggest that the day-to-day atmosphere of GTA 6's world will feel alive and unpredictable in ways that earlier entries simply couldn't manage.

## Why This Matters for Players

Weather in open-world games is often cosmetic, but systems this detailed can ripple through gameplay. Hurricanes, for example, could gate off certain areas, affect vehicle handling, or trigger specific mission conditions. Even something as simple as a rainbow after a storm can shift the mood and make the world feel more believable.

> Rockstar's stated goal is a "sense of scale we haven't felt before" — and a sky that behaves like a real Florida sky goes a long way toward delivering on that promise.
