## The Scale of What Rockstar Is Pulling Off

As GTA 6's November 2026 release approaches, one debate keeps resurfacing in fan communities: why isn't the base PS5 version targeting 60 frames per second? A thread on r/GTA6 recently pushed back hard against that criticism, and honestly, the argument is worth unpacking properly.

The PS5 launched back in late 2020. By the time GTA 6 ships, that hardware will be six years old. It was designed and priced as a mid-range consumer device, not a workstation. Expecting it to push a seamlessly simulated open world — reportedly the most detailed and expansive in Rockstar's history — at 60 FPS is a tall order regardless of how good the engine is.

## Why 30 FPS Isn't a Cop-Out

Achieving a *stable* 30 FPS is meaningfully different from a choppy or inconsistent one. A locked, well-paced 30 FPS with proper frame pacing can feel smooth and playable, especially on a large television at typical couch-viewing distances. What matters most is consistency, not just the raw number.

- GTA 6's world simulation — traffic, weather, NPC behaviour, physics — is far more demanding than anything in GTA V
- Rockstar has a strong track record of squeezing performance out of aging console hardware
- PC players and next-gen hardware owners will likely have higher frame-rate options down the line

> Keep in mind: none of the specific performance targets discussed here are officially confirmed by Rockstar. Treat community speculation as exactly that.

## What This Means for Players

If you're planning to play GTA 6 on a base PS5, a stable 30 FPS experience is a reasonable and respectable outcome given the hardware constraints. Managing expectations now means you can focus on enjoying what looks set to be a landmark release.
