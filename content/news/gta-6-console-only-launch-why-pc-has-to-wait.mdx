## PC Players Left on the Sidelines — For Now

When GTA 6 arrives on November 19, 2026, it will land exclusively on consoles. PC players already know this, but the *why* has always been a bit murky. Now, former Rockstar producer John Ricchio — who contributed to GTA 5, Red Dead Redemption, and Max Payne 3 — has offered some candid perspective on the reasoning behind that decision.

Ricchio is careful to stress that Rockstar isn't dismissing the PC audience. Far from it. His comments suggest the console-first approach is a deliberate, strategic choice rather than a sign of indifference toward PC gaming.

## The Logic Behind Console Priority

While Ricchio doesn't speak for Rockstar in any official capacity, his insider experience points to a few likely factors:

- **Controlled hardware environment** — Consoles offer a consistent, predictable platform, making it easier to ship a polished experience at launch without the enormous variable of optimising for thousands of different PC configurations.
- **Commercial momentum** — A console launch generates a concentrated sales spike and cultural buzz. A simultaneous PC release would split focus and resources during the most critical window.
- **PC as a second wave** — Rockstar has followed this pattern before. GTA 5 launched on PS3 and Xbox 360 in 2013, then came to PC in 2015. A PC version of GTA 6 is widely expected to follow, just not on day one.

> Ricchio's comments are his own perspective as a former employee — they are not an official statement from Rockstar Games or Take-Two Interactive.

## What This Means for PC Players

Patience is the only real option here. The good news is that a PC release, when it does arrive, tends to be the definitive version — higher frame rates, mod support, and improved visuals.
