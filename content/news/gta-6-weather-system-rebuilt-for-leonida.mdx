## A Weather Engine Built from the Ground Up

Rockstar hasn't just tweaked the weather for GTA 6 — according to the studio's vice president of art for lighting and rendering, Owen Shepherd, the entire system has been re-engineered. Speaking to Game Informer, Shepherd explained that the goal was to make weather feel "localized and persistent," giving the game's fictional Florida-inspired state of Leonida a sense of atmospheric scale the studio has never achieved before.

## What Florida Weather Actually Looks Like — and How GTA 6 Replicates It

Anyone who has spent time in Florida knows its weather is famously unpredictable. Intense sunshine in one neighbourhood, a drenching downpour a mile away. Rockstar has leaned into that reality:

- **Localized rain showers** can occur in one part of the map while the sun blazes elsewhere
- **Fully dynamic cloudscapes** roll across Leonida in real time, shifting the sky throughout the day
- **Rainbows** appear after showers pass, adding a natural, lived-in feel
- **Persistent wet surfaces and puddles** remain after rain, so the world doesn't instantly snap back to dry
- A **new wind system** has also been introduced, though full details on its scope are still emerging

> Shepherd's stated aim was for the weather to feel "dynamically solid and achieve a sense of scale" Rockstar has never delivered before — a bold claim from a studio known for pushing open-world boundaries.

## Why This Matters for Players

Beyond looking impressive, a genuinely reactive weather system has real gameplay implications. Wet roads could affect vehicle handling, reduced visibility during storms may change how you approach missions, and a living sky makes the [open world](/news) feel less like a backdrop and more like a place. With GTA 6 launching on November 19, 2026, Leonida's skies are shaping up to be one of the most dynamic in open-world gaming.
