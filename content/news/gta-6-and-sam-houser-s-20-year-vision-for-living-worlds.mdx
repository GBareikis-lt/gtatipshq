## A Vision Two Decades in the Making

A Reddit post on r/GTA6 recently spotlighted a 2005 interview with Rockstar co-founder Sam Houser, and fans have been quick to point out how strikingly relevant his words still sound today. In the interview, Houser laid out a core ambition: build game worlds that feel genuinely alive, while anchoring them to strong characters and meaningful stories. Reading it now, with GTA 6 on the horizon, it is hard not to see those early ideas as a blueprint that Rockstar has spent the better part of twenty years trying to perfect.

## From Concept to Leonida

Everything shown about GTA 6 so far suggests the team has pushed that living-world philosophy further than ever before:

- NPCs with dynamic routines and reactive behaviour
- A sprawling Florida-inspired map covering urban, coastal, and rural environments
- A narrative centred on two protagonists, with Rockstar clearly investing heavily in character depth
- Environmental storytelling baked into everyday details across the map

None of that happened overnight. It is the result of iterating on Red Dead Redemption 2's ecosystem systems and GTA Online's persistent world, layered on top of foundational design thinking that Houser was already articulating back when the PlayStation 2 was the dominant console.

## The Vice City Connection

The Reddit post also pairs the interview audio with Raf's 1984 track *Self Control* — the original version, as it turns out. Most GTA fans know the Laura Branigan cover from GTA: Vice City's radio stations, so hearing the source material woven into a GTA 6 tribute is a neat full-circle moment that underlines just how long Rockstar has been drawing on Miami's cultural identity.
