## Take-Two Acknowledged Serious Pricing Power

In a recent interview with CNBC, Take-Two Interactive CEO Strauss Zelnick made a candid admission: the company had more room to push GTA 6's price higher than it ultimately did. According to Zelnick, many observers believed Take-Two could have leveraged the enormous demand surrounding the game to set an even steeper launch price. Ultimately, leadership chose not to go that route.

GTA 6 launched with two price tiers:
- **Standard Edition** – $80
- **Premium Edition** – $100 (includes a bundle of digital extras)

Zelnick's stated reasoning was straightforward — Take-Two wanted to deliver value that clearly exceeded the asking price, and it did not want the game to feel financially out of reach for a broad audience.

## The $100 Edition Is Selling Well

Despite some initial sticker shock around the $80 base price, Zelnick noted that preorder data shows buyers are actually gravitating toward the more expensive $100 edition in notable numbers. That suggests a meaningful portion of the playerbase is willing to pay a premium when the extras feel worthwhile.

## The GTA Online Factor

There's a strategic angle worth considering here. Keeping the standard edition at $80 — rather than pushing into uncharted territory above $100 — likely lowers the barrier to entry for a larger pool of players. A bigger player base at launch feeds directly into GTA Online, which remains one of Take-Two's most important long-term revenue streams. Accessible upfront pricing and ongoing online monetisation have always worked hand-in-hand for Rockstar.

## What This Means for Players

For most buyers, the key takeaway is reassuring: Take-Two appears to have left money on the table deliberately, prioritising reach over short-term revenue. Whether the $80 entry point feels fair will ultimately depend on what the final package delivers.
